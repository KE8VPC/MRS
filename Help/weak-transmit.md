# FT8, FT4 and WSPR transmit
Context: Weak signal Amateur
Keywords: transmit FT8 FT4 WSPR CQ answer auto sequence General HackRF Halt timing output power

Start live HackRF USB reception on the desired amateur dial frequency. In Amateur → FT8 / FT4 / WSPR, select the mode and choose Transmit. A stable device identity is required. Other receiver families remain receive-only. The selected dial is fixed for the session; change it in the receiver before opening this window.

Enter your callsign, four-character Maidenhead grid, license class, audio offset and low TX gain. Confirm current station authority, your actual location/frequency/mode permissions, and connected equipment/output/filter compliance. This release supports a conservative subset of U.S. Region 2 amateur data segments. It does not verify a license or measure RF power. Receive presets and reference CSVs do not unlock transmission. Other jurisdictions, 60 m and LF/MF require separate policies and are not enabled.

Use fresh NIST timing, or explicitly confirm that you independently checked PC UTC synchronization. TX uses a monotonic clock anchored to that reference. Decoder-only manual/RX timing estimates are not used for transmission. The worker refuses a missed slot rather than starting late. Hardware USB startup, RF latency and oscillator accuracy still require an independent RF check. RX PPM calibration remains RX-only; it is not silently applied to TX. Measure transmit frequency and sample-clock stability before relying on weak-signal operation, especially narrow WSPR reception.

For FT8/FT4, choose Call CQ or enter a peer and choose Answer peer. A selected decode supplies the peer and an estimated signal report; inspect it before use. The peer's received slot determines the opposite transmit parity when a suitable decode was selected. Otherwise choose even/odd yourself. A CQ is a general call, not a callsign. Only resolved standard three-field exchanges are enabled; free text, hashed calls, contest/DXpedition formats and arbitrary messages are excluded.

Manual operation: choose Grid, Report, R-report, RR73 or 73 and Set manual exchange step. Arm sends one burst, then disarms for your review. Optional automatic advancement only accepts validated fresh live messages from the selected peer addressed to your callsign, on the same mode and dial. It follows grid → report → R-report → RR73/73 as appropriate for the side of the exchange. It does not automatically choose a caller in response to your CQ. Halt first, select/enter a peer, then answer.

FT8 slots are 15 seconds; FT4 slots are 7.5 seconds. This first half-duplex implementation permits an extra receive cycle between scheduled transmit slots so the complete receive window can decode before the HackRF handoff. It is slower than a mature dedicated transceiver workflow. A session is limited to 1–10 bursts and five minutes, with at most three repeats of an unchanged step. A final 73 or peer completion ends the exchange. No ADIF/contact logging or external CAT/PTT is included.

WSPR is one beacon per arm, not a QSO exchange. Enter the actual measured output power in supported 0/3/7 dBm steps, 0–37 dBm. Gain is not an output-power measurement. WSPR schedules a 120-second boundary, begins its waveform after the standard one-second lead and sends 162 symbols (about 110.6 seconds). There is a ten-minute cooldown for the same device while M.R.S. is running. No unattended repeat schedule is enabled.

Halt cancels the armed schedule and requests immediate worker stop. Closing the window also halts. Faults, missed slots, clock jumps, source changes, exhausted limits and failed native drains disarm. The RF amplifier and antenna power remain off. A bounded streaming 2 MS/s signed-I/Q generator avoids storing a multi-hundred-MB WSPR waveform. The worker has its own deadline and stdin/parent-loss stop. M.R.S. restores the prior USB receive channel and waits for acknowledgment after each burst; automatic/manual recordings stay disarmed. If restore fails, reception stays stopped and the error is shown.

Generated audio and the quantized I/Q path are checked locally. FT8, FT4 and WSPR generated samples have also decoded in the separately installed WSJT-X tools. These tests do not demonstrate actual RF timing, signal purity, received output or a completed live QSO. Begin with suitable non-radiating equipment and a separate monitor when performing your own hardware acceptance.
