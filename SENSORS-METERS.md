# Sensors, utility meters and burst inspection — 0.35.0-preview1

Open Analysis > Sensors / meters / bursts. Start a live SDR or I/Q replay, choose a profile and click Start analysis. All analysis is receive-only and uses the receiver already owned by M.R.S. It starts off on launch. Raw I/Q analysis is independent of listening mode, audio filter and audio squelch.

## Profiles

- Sensors / remotes: upstream rtl_433 default-enabled decoders. Intended first searches are 433.920 and 315.000 MHz; device-specific frequencies vary. The helper normally receives about 1.2 MS/s extracted around the listening frequency, or the available lower rate. Source selection and capture coverage are checked before processing.
- Utility meters: selected ERT SCM, SCMplus and IDM plus Badger ORION and Neptune R900 decoder families. A useful starting search is 911.600 or 912.600 MHz, but these devices use different/hopping channels across 902–928 MHz. The extracted span is normally about 2.4 MS/s. One capture does not cover the entire band. The profile reports raw consumption counters; do not assume kWh/gallons or ownership from an ID. IDM/netIDM interpretations can be ambiguous; the alternative netIDM parser is deliberately not enabled alongside IDM.
- Burst inspection / narrow NFM: no device decoder; extracts a narrower channel around the selected listening frequency and shows experimental envelope crossings, burst duration, min/median/max timings and strongest tested audio-frequency bins. The initial frequency bank includes 740, 996 and 1180 Hz for investigating the supplied utility load-control recordings. These are descriptive candidates, not recovered bits or decoded load-control commands.

HackRF starting configuration: 9.6 MS/s, existing working gains, RF amplifier off unless needed, HF hardware filter off for broad sensor/meter profiles. A +250 kHz capture offset fits at that rate. At 2.4 MS/s the meter span plus offset may not fit: disable/reduce offset or increase rate. The analysis status explains coverage failures. Lower sample rates can reduce CPU load for sensors; meter bandwidth requirements remain separate.

Tune presets request a normal receiver tune and stop analysis; click Start analysis afterward. A chosen profile, burst margin and digital decoder gain apply at Start. Start gain at +0 dB; try +12/+24 dB for weak filtered sensor/meter signals, reducing it if clipping prevents decoding. This gain changes only the helper input, not hardware gain, listening audio or recorded I/Q. The inspector retains floating-point filtered samples to avoid losing faint bursts to 8-bit rounding. Selecting a results subtab only changes which observations are visible, not the running profile. Opening a new source, tuning/rate/gain/mode change or replay seek resets decoder state. Source completion processes queued data, then stops; reconnect does not silently resume it. Stop analysis cancels workers; unrelated decoder workspaces continue.

## Results and labels

Sensor and meter views show model, decoder ID, raw measurements and units where provided, backend integrity wording, received-message count, last-seen UTC and source. Counts are received messages, not physical device transmissions; identical retransmissions can increase counts. Select a row for raw JSON; no raw bits are fabricated when upstream only supplies fields. Labels persist per frequency/model/ID in the receiver's sensor-labels.json and do not replace decoded IDs. Live and replay observations remain separate. A missing integrity field explicitly says unspecified.

The list retains up to 500 grouped device observations and 100 recent burst records. The burst waveform shows at most 2,048 envelope intervals per segment. Long bursts are segmented at five seconds or 4,096 transitions; weak/fading activity can also split into multiple segments. Narrow inspection uses a 50 ms quiet tail; other profiles use 10 ms. Raw JSON export includes retained observations, aliases and pulse timing. It is a snapshot, not an indefinite database. Unknown device IDs do not establish a physical source. Clear removes observations, not saved labels. Burst threshold is an estimated quiet-floor plus the selected margin; it is separate from receiver squelch and is not calibrated dBm. Activity gating uses integrated power rather than individual noise peaks; narrow inspection also smooths its displayed envelope. Allow approximately 100 ms of quiet for initial calibration; a carrier present from startup can prevent reliable activity triggering.

The inspector is an initial OOK/envelope and tone-analysis tool, not a full symbol editor or a universal FSK/PSK/SCADA decoder. Rapid pulses can be smoothed by its envelope estimate. Tone values are only the strongest bins in a fixed test bank, not an unrestricted frequency estimate or a confidence score. Original .mrsiq recordings remain the evidence for further decoder work.

## Boundaries and validation

The pinned rtl_433 source, local stdin-binary fix, native I/Q bridge source, licensing and hashes are in Decoders/Sensors. Docs/rtl433-catalog.json lists the pinned example-configuration protocol inventory; it is a reference, not a list of enabled or locally validated devices. No Mayhem embedded app or rtlamr binary is shipped. Public AcuRite, ERT SCM, SCMplus and IDM recordings were used for offline validation; this does not establish reception of every supported model. The original 154.463 MHz utility load-control encoding remains unidentified. DNP3/Modbus, encrypted meter payloads, Remote ID, FPV video and P25/DMR trunk following are not added by this feature.
